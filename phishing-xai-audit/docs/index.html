Beyond Accuracy: Auditing Duplicates, Baselines and Explanation Faithfulness in Transformer-Based Phishing Email Detection

A public phishing-email corpus reports accuracy above 98%. We check what that number rests on: duplicate and placeholder emails, the strength of the baselines, and whether word-level explanations survive a deletion test.

What we found

The paper is an audit, not a new detector. It builds on Uddin et al. (2026) and uses the same corpus (18,631 emails after cleaning) and model family. The findings below are the paper's own; the paper states every limit.

  1. RQ1

    The benchmark has a ceiling, and a second "independent" dataset is a mirror.

    One placeholder text, "empty", appears 533 times with both labels (335 phishing, 198 safe). Any text-only classifier must get the minority copies wrong, so raw-split accuracy cannot exceed about 98.9%. A second public dataset overlaps the Kaggle corpus by 99.8% in our sample (2,993 of 3,000 emails), so it is not a transfer test.

  2. RQ2

    On full text, transformers and TF-IDF linear models are statistically tied.

    After de-duplication, accuracy is 98.58% (DistilRoBERTa), 98.84% (RoBERTa-base), 98.65% (SVM) and 98.59% (LR), averaged over three seeds. When the linear models see only the transformers' first 256 tokens, RoBERTa-base leads by up to 0.9 points, and 2 of its 12 paired comparisons stay significant after Holm correction across 24.

  3. RQ3

    Which explainer looks best depends on how you configure it.

    On RoBERTa-base, Integrated Gradients with the default pad baseline scores like a random word order (comprehensiveness 0.006 against 0.003). With a mask baseline it reaches 0.077, level with LIME (0.073). SHAP's score on DistilRoBERTa rose 2.5-fold when its evaluation budget grew from 300 to 2,000. Re-sampling LIME on fixed emails moved the score at most 1.4-fold, while two full pipeline runs differed up to 4.7-fold; the paper does not separate sample and checkpoint effects.

  4. RQ4

    Homoglyphs trigger false alarms in the transformers, and truncation does not explain it.

    Replacing 15% of some letters with Cyrillic look-alikes raised DistilRoBERTa's false-positive rate from 2.3% to 7.2% and RoBERTa-base's from 0.8% to 2.0%, while TF-IDF LR moved from 1.2% to 1.4%. A 512-token limit and a short-email subset gave the same pattern. Typos and zero-width characters changed F1 by at most 0.55 points.

The accuracy gap is inside the noise

Mean accuracy on the deduplicated test sets with one standard deviation over seeds 42, 43 and 44 (Table II of the paper). The four non-forest models overlap.

Random forest (97.02 ± 0.10) is the only model clearly worse. Paired McNemar tests on the same 3,499 emails: RoBERTa-base against LR p = 0.31, against SVM p = 0.52.

Method and figures from the paper

Faithfulness here means the ERASER deletion test: delete the top-k words an explainer ranks highest and measure how far the model's probability for its original prediction falls. The predicted class is held fixed.

Try the explanations on an email

The app/ folder holds a small Gradio demo. Paste an email and it shows the prediction, word highlights from LIME or Integrated Gradients (both baselines), and a deletion check on the predicted class. Model weights are not in the repository: point the app at a fine-tuned checkpoint you trained with the notebook.

pip install -r app/requirements.txt
MODEL_DIR=/path/to/model_distilroberta-base python app/app.py

The demo was tested end to end on a tiny locally trained model, not yet on the paper's checkpoints.

Reproduce the results

You need a free Colab T4 GPU and the Kaggle file Phishing_Email.csv (dataset subhajournal/phishingemails) in MyDrive/phish_xai. Datasets are not redistributed.

  1. notebooks/01_main_experiments.ipynb trains the models and runs explainers, faithfulness and robustness.
  2. scripts/controls_colab.py runs paired tests, overlap, length and IG controls, and the SHAP budget test.
  3. scripts/audit_checks.py runs the duplicate and leakage diagnostics (CPU only).
  4. notebooks/02_round2_experiments.ipynb runs tuned and matched baselines, near-duplicate splits, LIME repeats and the stratified sample.
  5. Rebuild the paper from the saved results with paper/build/build_paper.py. No GPU needed.

All numbers in the paper come from the CSV files in results/.

Limits

Differences of a few tenths of a point between models are within seed variation on this corpus. Read the paper's limitations section before quoting any figure.

  • Three seeds for most results; one seed for the near-duplicate group splits with transformers.
  • Explanation tests use 100 emails per model and a stratified sample of 120, not the whole test set.
  • One public corpus plus a 3,000-email Enron-Spam sample, which is spam rather than phishing and overlaps the corpus (34%).
  • For the raw split, the transformers' errors on the "empty" rows were not checked email by email, so the overall effect of duplicate overlap is left unresolved.
  • The deletion test creates off-distribution inputs, which has its own confound.
